The clock

Time-critical

Reprocessing of the entire EMIT catalog begins around September 2026 and takes roughly 75 days, regenerating every mineral map against Tetracorder 6 with updated reflectance. The mineral classes shift.

For those ~10 weeks the archive is mixed-vintage. Any run that does not pin a vintage will silently blend two incompatible products into a plausible-looking result. Nothing in the data announces the problem.

This is why vintage pinning is mandatory rather than advisory, why build_version is an indexed first-class column, and why plan-time validation refuses a frozen index spanning multiple vintages unless the manifest explicitly opts in.

The first implementation slice

Deliberately narrow, and chosen so that finishing it proves the abstraction rather than the plumbing.

Scope

One tile, one epoch, MinViewZenith, staged granules on local disk, output diffed against a V002 cell. No blocks, caching, Step Functions, Batch, or manifest patching.

Build order within it: types → L2B reader → regrid wrapper → resolve → publish.

The success criterion is parity, not novelty. If a streaming scorer with a single epoch and a no-op reducer does not reproduce V002 exactly, the abstraction is wrong — and it is far cheaper to discover that now than after the orchestration is built on top of it.

The first concrete deliverable inside that slice is the L2B mineral reader. spec_io.open_netcdf dispatches on filename substrings and has readers for EMIT rdn, rfl, obs and l2a_mask plus airborne sensors — but none for the L2B mineral products, which are the primary Critical Minerals input. An EMIT_L2B_MIN_*.nc currently raises ValueError: Unknown file type.

A second consumer already exists

Colorado School of Mines — with CMU and U. Wisconsin’s Macrostrat — is independently building the same thing: tiling Tetracorder mineral maps into state- and country-wide mosaics, with a GUI and a QGIS data stream, moving to AVIRIS-5, and running Tetracorder on a supercomputer via a CMU wrapper. Their cross-scale ambition runs from EMIT’s 60 m through AVIRIS at ~60 cm to UAV imagery at 6–10 cm.

That makes “a framework, not an EMIT program” a present requirement rather than an aspiration, and it is why input roles and band aliases resolve per collection rather than being hard-coded. They independently confirmed two of our findings: QGIS is the working visualization path, and earthaccess is the access mechanism.

One open question from their side worth carrying: why does NASA distribute ten per-mineral abundance maps rather than a single classified map? That is the collection-layout question arriving from outside, and it suggests both representations have real users.

Attribution caveat

The tag-up record is voice-derived and speaker attribution is unreliable. Claims in the notes are attributed by content rather than by transcript headers, timestamps are given so each can be checked, and anything load-bearing should be confirmed before it is acted on.
